\section{The Mixing Board: LLM-Driven Reward Shaping}
\label{ch:dynamic_implementation}

This section details the first integration strategy: LLM-driven dynamic reward shaping, informally referred to as the \emph{Mixing Board} approach.
Intuitively, this approach separates \emph{strategy} from \emph{execution}. Returning to our established metaphor, the LLM acts as the Commander standing at a strategic mixing board, adjusting priority dials for various subtasks. Meanwhile, the low-level PPO agents (the lumberjack and miner)~\parencite{Yu_Velu_Vinitsky_Gao_Wang_Bayen_Wu_2022} act as the kinetic workers, navigating the physical grid-world to fulfill whichever priority is currently dialed highest.

The Commander does not act directly inside the environment. Instead, it periodically observes the agents' progress and adjusts a vector of subtask priority weights. These weights govern two things:
(i) which semantic goal the agents' internal planner selects, and
(ii) the magnitude of the gravitational pull (potential-based shaping signal) guiding the agents toward that goal.

This paradigm aligns with a growing body of work using LLMs as meta-level reward designers for RL.
For instance, EUREKA~\parencite{ma2024eurekahumanlevelrewarddesign} demonstrated that an LLM can write and iteratively refine reward functions that exceed human design.
Similarly, PRM4RL~\parencite{Zhang_Gao_Jiang_Chen_Pan_Zhang_Deng_2025} uses LLMs to decompose tasks and generate dense shaping rewards.
More recently, MEDS~\parencite{liu2026memoryenhanceddynamic} introduced memory-enhanced dynamic shaping to penalize redundant behaviors, while GLARE~\parencite{yan2026glare} proposed translating trajectory events into temporal logic formulas for dense reward assignment.

The present work shares this motivation but operates at a much faster, latency-bound granularity. Rather than writing entirely new reward functions from scratch, our Commander simply adjusts a \emph{vector of priority weights} that steer goal selection within a fixed, mathematically safe potential-based shaping framework.

The exposition of this chapter follows the exact chronological dataflow of the implementation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{DAG-Conditional Planning} (\cref{sec:dag_planner}): The system prunes impossible tasks based on the agents' current inventory, preventing the Commander from issuing invalid orders.
    \item \textbf{LLM Weight Query} (\cref{sec:llm_weight_query}): The Commander is queried to assign raw semantic priorities to the remaining valid subtasks.
    \item \textbf{Softmax Budget Normalisation} (\cref{sec:softmax_budget}): The Commander's raw priorities are mathematically smoothed into a rigorously bounded probability distribution.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Based Reward Shaping} (\cref{sec:pbrs}): The normalized budget is injected into the training loop as a dense, magnetic reward signal to guide the workers.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{System Overview}
\label{sec:dyn_overview}

\Cref{fig:dyn_system_flow} illustrates the end-to-end pipeline of the Mixing Board architecture.
Conceptually, the system operates in a continuous loop. At every standard environment step, the agents update their inventory, the DAG Planner assigns them a valid target, and the PBRS module feeds them a shaped reward to guide their movement.

However, at the end of every training update, a \emph{Critic Trigger} acts as a diagnostic monitor. It watches the agents' learning curves (TD-error and success rates). If the trigger detects that the lumberjack and miner are stuck at a learning plateau (spinning their wheels without making progress), it halts the system. It constructs a prompt detailing the failure, queries the LLM Commander for a new strategic direction, filters that new direction through a safety layer (\texttt{RewardInjector}), and updates the planner to pull the agents out of their rut.

\subsection{DAG-Conditional Planner}
\label{sec:dag_planner}

Before the LLM's weights can be applied, we must ensure the Commander cannot assign impossible tasks. The crafting environment enforces a strict dependency graph over subtasks, as established by the topological task progression in \Cref{fig:env_dag}.

\subsubsection{Greedy Planner and Base Rewards}

For each parallel environment, the planner acts as a logical filter. It inspects the agents' current shared inventory vector $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{10}$ and builds a set of \emph{feasible} subtasks, denoted as $\mathcal{F}^{(i)}(\mathbf{x})$.
For example, Agent~0 (the Lumberjack) might be restricted to targeting \{Wood, Workbench, Bridge, Enemy\}, while Agent~1 (the Miner) might target \{Stone, Workbench, Iron, Enemy, Gold\}.
Crucially, a subtask only enters this feasible set if its DAG preconditions are met; if the agents have not yet crafted a Pickaxe ($\mathbf{x}_{\text{pickaxe}} = 0$), then mining Iron is mathematically masked out of the options~\parencite{Albrecht_Christianos_Schäfer_2024}.

Once the impossible tasks are pruned, the planner must decide which of the valid tasks to pursue. It does this by calculating a \emph{score} $s_j$ for each feasible subtask $j$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:greedy_score}
    s_j = R_j^{\text{base}} \cdot w_j
\end{equation}
This score is the product of two distinct forces:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Intrinsic Difficulty ($R_j^{\text{base}}$):} A hand-crafted base reward that encodes the structural depth of the task. Deep-tree tasks receive high base rewards (e.g., $R_{\text{gold}}=15$) because they are harder to reach, while trivial early tasks receive low rewards (e.g., $R_{\text{wood}}=1$).
    \item \textbf{Commander Urgency ($w_j$):} The dynamic multiplier provided by the LLM. If the base rewards were uniformly $1.0$, the LLM would have to output extreme, destabilizing weights to convince an agent to walk past a trivial piece of wood to mine deep-game gold. The base reward provides the natural gravity, and the LLM simply tweaks the dial.
\end{enumerate}

The agent then greedily selects the subtask with the highest resulting score to be its active goal $g^{(i)}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:greedy_select}
    g^{(i)} = \arg\max_{j \in \mathcal{F}^{(i)}(\mathbf{x})}\;
              R_j^{\text{base}} \cdot w_j
\end{equation}

Crucially, while the planner only selects the single maximum value to dictate the agent's immediate target, the remaining weights are not permanently discarded. Because the system employs time-smoothing (detailed in \cref{sec:ema_smoothing}), a lower-ranked weight might continuously rise across subsequent timesteps until it ultimately overtakes the current leader, ensuring smooth and stable goal transitions.

In practice, evaluating this logic across 128 parallel training environments requires strict vectorisation. This is handled by the Python backend which computes these conditional scores dynamically for each agent.

\subsubsection{Cooperative Coupling Guardrail}

Because the environment is fully cooperative, certain tasks require synchronized presence. If either agent selects a cooperative bottleneck task (such as returning to the Workbench or building the Bridge), the planner overrides individual greedy selections and magnetically pulls the partner agent to the same target, ensuring they co-locate to trigger the interaction.

\subsubsection{DAG Guardrails}

Finally, before the Commander's weight vector is even allowed to touch the planner, it must pass through a hard programmatic filter (\texttt{apply\_dag\_guardrails}). While LLMs are powerful semantic reasoners, they are prone to hallucinations. If the LLM assigns a high priority to mining Iron ($w_{\text{iron}} > 0$) when no pickaxe exists, it would inject catastrophic noise into the PPO value network. This programmatic guardrail acts as an absolute constraint layer, zeroing out hallucinated weights and overriding the LLM output to maintain mathematical stability.

\subsection{Two-Stage Critic Trigger}
\label{sec:critic_trigger}

If the LLM Commander were to issue new orders at every single step, the system would collapse under inference latency, and the PPO agents would never have the stable environment necessary to learn low-level kinetic tasks. Therefore, the Commander only intervenes when the agents are genuinely stuck. The system determines this using a mathematical \emph{two-stage gate} evaluated at the end of each PPO update iteration.

\subsubsection{Stage~1: The Plateau Gate (Production Stalled)}

First, the system monitors the agents' actual production rate. A rolling window of the last $W$ success-rate values is maintained. The first gate opens only when the improvement in task completion over the previous window falls below a negligible threshold~$\epsilon$:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:plateau_gate}
    \text{gate\_open}
    \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
    \left|\;
        \underbrace{\frac{1}{W}\sum_{k=0}^{W-1}
            \rho_{t-k}}_{\bar{\rho}_{\text{recent}}}
        \;-\;
        \underbrace{\frac{1}{W}\sum_{k=W}^{2W-1}
            \rho_{t-k}}_{\bar{\rho}_{\text{older}}}
    \;\right|
    \;<\;
    \epsilon
\end{equation}

where $\rho_t$ is the success rate at update~$t$, $W$ is the \texttt{plateau\_window} (default~10), and $\epsilon$ is the \texttt{plateau\_threshold} (default~0.02). $W$ acts as a low-pass filter on the inherently noisy RL training signal. It ensures the Commander is not bothered by a single bad batch of trajectories, but only alerted when the smoothed trend shows that the lumberjack and miner have completely stalled. Additionally, this gate opens if any specific individual subtask has plateaued (variance $< 0.02$ over 20~updates while remaining between 5\% and 95\% completion).

\subsubsection{Stage~2: The Z-Score Trigger (The SOS Flare)}

Just because production has stalled does not mean the agents need new orders; they might simply be navigating a physically long path. To confirm they are actually stuck, the system looks at the Centralized Critic.

As established in \cref{subsec:td_error}, the TD-Error (Temporal Difference Error) measures the ``surprise gap'' of the Critic network—the mathematical difference between its prior prediction and the newly observed reality. A high \emph{mean} TD-error is not necessarily alarming; it merely indicates that the environment has a large reward scale. 

However, a spike in TD-error \emph{variance} is a critical failure metric. It means the Critic is fundamentally confused: it is seeing wildly different returns for the exact same states (e.g., sometimes observing a massive reward, and sometimes a penalty for identical actions). This variance spike occurs when agents get stuck at a bottleneck, thrash between uncoordinated goals, or experience severe non-stationarity~\parencite{Foerster_Nardelli_Farquhar_Afouras_Torr_Kohli_Whiteson_2017}. Therefore, variance acts as the mathematical SOS flare of a confused agent. Once the plateau gate is open, the system checks whether the Critic's TD-error variance has spiked beyond $\sigma$ standard deviations from its running mean:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:zscore_trigger}
    z = \frac{
        \text{Var}[\delta_t] - \overline{\text{Var}[\delta]}
    }{
        \text{Std}\bigl[\text{Var}[\delta]\bigr]
    }
    \;>\;
    \frac{\sigma_{\text{base}}}{\max(\eta,\, 0.1)}
\end{equation}

where $\delta_t$ is the TD error at update~$t$, the bar denotes the running mean over a window of 100 updates, $\sigma_{\text{base}}=1.5$, and $\eta$ is a sensitivity factor that decays multiplicatively by $0.995$ after each trigger, raising the effective threshold over time to prevent over-triggering.

If the gate is open and the Z-score trigger fires, an LLM intervention is dispatched to rescue the agents, followed by a cooldown of 25~updates to give the new orders time to take effect.

\subsection{LLM Weight Query: Continuous Logit Output}
\label{sec:llm_weight_query}

When the SOS flare fires, the \texttt{PromptBuilder} constructs a structured situation report for the Commander. This prompt includes:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item The trigger reason (plateau description, z-score value).
    \item Current subtask completion percentages, with saturation flags ($\ge 95\%$).
    \item Per-agent TD-error statistics (mean, variance, per-agent breakdown).
    \item Current reward shaping weights.
    \item An episodic memory buffer of the last 3 interventions and their resulting TD-error deltas.
\end{enumerate}

Crucially, the prompt explicitly instructs the LLM to output \emph{unnormalized log-probabilities (logits)}, rather than final strict weights:

\begin{quote}
\small\sffamily
``Your output values represent unnormalized log-probabilities (logits).
They will be processed via a temperature-scaled softmax function and
scaled to maintain a constant total reward budget.
Focus on the RELATIVE MAGNITUDE and PRIORITY RANKING of subtasks.''
\end{quote}

This design leverages the synergy between reasoning and acting~\parencite{Yao_Zhao_Yu_Du_Shafran_Narasimhan_Cao_2023}. It intentionally decouples the LLM from absolute mathematical calibration, allowing it to act purely as a meta-supervisor~\parencite{Sun_Huang_Pompili_2024}. The Commander is free to express priority differences via wide "feelings" of importance (e.g., scoring a critical bottleneck at 8.0 and a completed task at 0.5), knowing the downstream math will safely normalize this into a stable reward budget.

Furthermore, although the planner ultimately selects a single target goal (\cref{eq:greedy_select}), outputting these continuous logit values is essential for mathematical stability. If we forced the LLM to output binary $0/1$ weights, the planner would instantly snap to a new goal like flipping a light switch. This violent shift would disrupt the Critic's value predictions and risk catastrophic forgetting. By using continuous logits, the system can blend the weights over time, transitioning the agents smoothly toward the new objective.

The LLM responds with a standardized JSON object containing its reasoning and the raw logits for each subtask:

\begin{hegquote}
\begin{verbatim}
{
  "reasoning": "<1-2 sentences>",
  "w_wood":      <float, logit>,
  "w_stone":     <float, logit>,
  "w_workbench": <float, logit>,
  "w_iron":      <float, logit>,
  "w_bridge":    <float, logit>,
  "w_enemy":     <float, logit>,
  "w_gold":      <float, logit>
}
\end{verbatim}
\end{hegquote}

\subsection{Softmax Attention Budget}
\label{sec:softmax_budget}

When the Commander responds to the SOS flare, the raw logits $\boldsymbol{c}=(c_1,\dots,c_K)$ it returns are unconstrained real numbers (e.g., they can be wildly positive or negative). If we applied these raw values directly to the reward function, the Commander could accidentally inflate or deflate the entire "economy" of the grid-world, completely destabilizing the PPO Critic's value estimations.

To prevent this, the raw logits are converted into a \emph{budget-preserving} weight vector via a temperature-scaled softmax, analogous to Boltzmann action selection~\parencite{Sutton_Barto_2018}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:softmax_budget}
\boxed{
    w_i
    \;=\;
    K \;\cdot\;
    \frac{
        \exp\!\bigl(c_i / \tau\bigr)
    }{
        \displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{K}
        \exp\!\bigl(c_j / \tau\bigr)
    }
}
\end{equation}
where $K=|\mathcal{W}|=7$ is the number of subtask weights and $\tau=0.5$ is the temperature hyperparameter. This operation enforces the following properties:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Constant budget}: $\sum_{i=1}^{K} w_i = K$ always. The total amount of priority distributed among the tasks remains perfectly constant, ensuring the overall reward magnitude is invariant to the LLM's output scale.
    \item \emph{Sharpening via temperature}: The exponentiation ($e^x$) forces the unconstrained logits into strictly positive values, creating a probability budget. The temperature $\tau$ acts as a "decisiveness" dial. As $\tau \to \infty$, the weights become perfectly uniform (ignoring the LLM). As $\tau \to 0$, the entire budget concentrates on the top choice, creating harsh transitions. A temperature of $\tau=0.5$ amplifies the Commander's priority differences while maintaining a continuous landscape suitable for time-smoothing.
    \item \emph{Numerical stability}: To prevent overflow errors during exponentiation, the implementation shifts the logits by subtracting the maximum value before applying the softmax.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{EMA Smoothing}
\label{sec:ema_smoothing}

As noted in Section~\ref{sec:dag_planner}, sudden transitions in target goals cause severe whiplash for the PPO algorithms. To ensure the active target shifts smoothly rather than snapping instantly, the softmax output is blended with the previously active weights via an Exponential Moving Average (EMA):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ema}
    \widetilde{w}_i^{(t)}
    \;=\;
    \alpha \cdot w_i^{\text{softmax}}
    + (1-\alpha) \cdot \widetilde{w}_i^{(t-1)}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha=0.2$. This mechanism acts as a shock absorber. It ensures that each new directive from the Commander takes roughly $\lceil 1/\alpha \rceil = 5$ updates to fully propagate into the environment, giving the agents time to adjust their trajectories.

\subsubsection{Safety Bounds and Auto-Rollback}
\label{sec:safety_rollback}

While the softmax budget normalizes the outputs, the system employs two final safety nets to enforce principles of Safe Reinforcement Learning and protect the workers from malformed LLM directives, a well-documented vulnerability often leading to reward hacking \parencite{Amodei_Olah_Steinhardt_Christiano_Schulman_Mané_2016}. 

First, before softmax normalization, individual logits are clipped to a maximum of $w_{\max}=3.0$, and the raw sum is bounded to $W_{\text{bound}}=15.0$. This prevents a hallucinated, extreme LLM logit from bypassing the temperature smoothing.

Second, the \texttt{RewardInjector} monitors the average environment reward for 25 updates following any LLM intervention. If this post-intervention average drops more than 20\% below the pre-intervention level, it triggers an \emph{Auto-Rollback}:
\begin{equation}
    \text{rollback}
    \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
    \frac{
        \bar{r}_{\text{pre}} - \bar{r}_{\text{post}}
    }{
        |\bar{r}_{\text{pre}}|
    }
    > 0.20
\end{equation}
Fundamental policy optimization algorithms, including PPO, are designed around the mathematical desire for monotonic improvement, guaranteeing that a new policy performs at least as well as the old one~\parencite{Schulman_Wolski_Dhariwal_Radford_Klimov_2017}. Because the LLM injects external, non-stationary shocks into the reward landscape, mathematical guarantees of monotonic improvement are temporarily broken. This Auto-Rollback acts as an empirical failsafe, instantly reverting the weights to restore training stability if the Commander's new strategy proves disastrous on the ground.

\subsection{Potential-Based Reward Shaping}
\label{sec:pbrs}

Once the planner evaluates the smoothed weights and assigns an active goal zone $g^{(i)}_t$ to agent~$i$, the environment must translate that goal into a physical force. This is achieved via the potential-based reward shaping (PBRS) framework established in Section~\ref{subsec:pbrs}.

\subsubsection{The Magnetic Pull (Potential Function)}
The potential of agent~$i$ in state~$s$ with respect to its assigned goal zone~$g$ is calculated geometrically:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:potential}
    \Phi^{(i)}(s, g) = D_{\max} - \|\mathbf{p}^{(i)}(s) - \mathbf{z}_g\|_2
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{p}^{(i)}(s)\in\mathbb{R}^2$ is the agent's $(x,y)$ position, $\mathbf{z}_g\in\mathbb{R}^2$ is the target zone's position, and $D_{\max}=85$ is the maximum diagonal distance of the grid. This potential is a strict measure of proximity: it is high when the agent is physically close to the goal and low when it is far away.

\subsubsection{Applying the Shaping Reward}
As the agent steps from state $t$ to $t+1$, the per-step shaping reward measures the difference in this potential:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shaping_reward}
    F_t^{(i)}
    \;=\;
    \bigl(\gamma\,\Phi^{(i)}(s_{t+1}, g) - \Phi^{(i)}(s_t, g)\bigr)
    \;\cdot\;
    \lambda_{\text{scale}}
    \;\cdot\;
    \mathds{1}[g \text{ active}]
\end{equation}
As established in Section~\ref{subsec:pbrs}, $\gamma=0.99$ acts as the anti-farming mechanism, mathematically punishing the agent if it loops backward along its own path.

The scale factor ($\lambda_{\text{scale}} = 0.05$) acts as a volume knob. If an agent runs a long distance across the map, its potential $\Phi$ might jump by $+20$ points. If unscaled, the agent would receive $+20$ simply for running, making marathon sprinting more lucrative than actually mining Gold (which only yields a true sparse reward of $+15$). Multiplying by $0.05$ shrinks that $+20$ movement reward down to a $+1.0$ nudge. This guarantees the dense shaping reward remains a gentle guide, while the sparse environmental milestones remain the ultimate, most profitable prize.

Additionally, the indicator $\mathds{1}[g \text{ active}]$ acts as a mask. If the agent completes its portion of the DAG and is idling while waiting for its partner, this mask zeroes out the shaping reward entirely, preventing the agent from being incorrectly penalized for remaining stationary.

To execute this equation simultaneously across 128 parallel environments without crushing the CPU, the shaping logic relies entirely on vectorized NumPy tensor operations, bypassing standard Python loops.

\subsubsection{Design Decision: Isolating Strategy from Force}
\label{sec:no_f_scaling}

You might notice that the LLM's priority weights $w_j$ do not appear anywhere in Equation~\ref{eq:shaping_reward}. This is an intentional, critical design decision. The shaping signal $F$ is \textbf{not} multiplied by the LLM's weights.

The Commander's influence is encapsulated entirely within the goal selection variable $g$ (\cref{eq:greedy_select}). The LLM dictates \emph{which} direction the magnetic pull originates from, but it does not change the \emph{strength} of that pull. If $F$ were dynamically scaled by the shifting LLM weights $w_j$, the underlying physical laws of the grid-world would constantly change, creating massive non-stationarity that would crash the PPO Critic. By isolating the strategy (the target $g$) from the force (the stable, policy-invariant gradient $F$), the agents can learn to navigate safely.

\subsubsection{Mathematical Impact on the MAPPO Objective}
\label{sec:dyn_math_impact}

In this Mixing Board implementation, the LLM does not alter the Actor network's state observations. The agents remain completely blind to the fact that they are being guided by an LLM; their policy remains strictly $\pi_\theta(a_t \vert{} s_t)$.

Instead, the LLM alters the environment's reward signal $r_t$ by injecting the shaping reward $F_t^{(i)}$ (scaled by a time-varying decay factor $\lambda_t$):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:total_reward}
    r_t^{\text{total}, (i)} = r_t^{\text{env}} + \lambda_t F_t^{(i)}
\end{equation}

This augmented reward is fed directly into the MAPPO Advantage calculation:
\begin{equation}
    A_t^{(i)} = r_t^{\text{total}, (i)} + \gamma V_\phi(s_{t+1}) - V_\phi(s_t)
\end{equation}

Consequently, the Centralized Critic network $V_\phi(s_t)$ adjusts its value estimations to predict this newly dense, goal-oriented reward landscape. The Actor simply follows the updated Advantage gradient $A_t^{(i)}$. The LLM acts strictly as an invisible architect, magnetically pulling the MAPPO policy toward specific regions of the state space without interfering with the neural network's architecture.

\subsubsection{Shaping Decay ($\lambda_t$)}
To ensure the agents eventually learn to act independently, the blending coefficient $\lambda_t$ from Equation~\ref{eq:total_reward} forces the shaping signal to fade out over time. 
The decay $\lambda_t$ begins at 1.0 and anneals linearly to 0.01 over 500 updates, but only \emph{after} the agents achieve a 40\% success rate on the critical "Bridge" bottleneck. This schedule ensures the agents have dense "training wheels" early in the learning process, and gracefully transitions them to the sparse environment reality once they prove they understand the map's topology.

\subsection{Async Communication Architecture}
\label{sec:async_bridge}

In standard Markov Decision Processes, observation and action delays severely destabilize training~\parencite{Katsikopoulos_Engelbrecht_2003}. If the PPO workers (the lumberjack and miner) had to pause and wait for the Commander's radio transmission (LLM inference) every time an SOS flare was fired, the simulation would bottleneck. Querying the LLM takes seconds due to network latency and inference time. If executed on the main thread, the GPU would sit at 0\% utilization, completely halting the MAPPO training loop.

To resolve this, queries are dispatched through an asynchronous \texttt{LLMBridge} module, which runs a background daemon worker thread. This asynchronous daemon approach mirrors recent LLM-agent frameworks like GITM~\parencite{Zhu_etal_2023}, which explicitly decouple the environment clock to accommodate LLM inference latency.

The asynchronous dataflow executes as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Trigger:} The Critic trigger fires at the end of a PPO update and constructs the prompt (the situation report).
    \item \textbf{Dispatch:} The prompt is enqueued to a thread-safe \texttt{queue.Queue}.
    \item \textbf{Background Processing:} A daemon worker thread dequeues the prompt and calls the LLM backend (Ollama HTTP API, HuggingFace local, or Gemini API) without interrupting the main simulation.
    \item \textbf{Continuous Execution:} While the LLM is "thinking," the RL loop continues gathering new trajectories using the \emph{old} priority weights.
    \item \textbf{Seamless Injection:} On completion, the callback parses the JSON, passes it to the \texttt{RewardInjector}, and stores it as ``pending weights.'' At the \emph{next} training update, the \texttt{CriticTrigger.on\_update\_end} detects the pending weights, applies the EMA smoothing, and updates the magnetic pull for the next rollout.
\end{enumerate}

This design guarantees that the high-frequency PPO update is never blocked by network latency. Crucially, this delay does not destabilize the agents because the Commander is only shifting the \emph{macro-strategy} (the reward gravity), rather than controlling their direct micro-actions. The LLM bridge also maintains a thread-safe response cache to avoid redundant queries for identical prompts, and utilizes an exponential backoff retry logic ($2^k$ seconds for attempt~$k$, up to 3~retries) to handle API timeouts safely.

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:dyn_summary}

The Mixing Board approach successfully treats the LLM as a \emph{meta-level Commander} that reads aggregate training statistics and outputs strategic priority logits. By routing these priorities through a cascade of safety layers (DAG guardrails, clipping, softmax budget normalization, EMA smoothing, and auto-rollback), the system ensures that the LLM's influence remains bounded and mathematically stable. Finally, the Potential-Based Reward Shaping (PBRS) framework guarantees that this strategic influence acts as a policy-invariant magnetic pull, guiding the workers out of exploration bottlenecks without corrupting the foundational reinforcement learning mechanics.
