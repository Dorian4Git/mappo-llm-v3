\section{Background: Large Language Models}
\label{ch:background_llm}

This chapter introduces the theoretical foundations of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their optimization mechanisms. It establishes how these autoregressive models can be adapted via parameter-efficient fine-tuning and subsequently integrated with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) as high-level cognitive controllers.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Foundations of Causal Language Modeling}
\label{sec:llm_foundations}

Unlike standard reinforcement learning policies that output continuous action vectors or low-dimensional discrete distributions, LLMs operate over massive discrete vocabularies (often exceeding $100,000$ unique tokens). These models rely entirely on the Transformer architecture, dispensing with classical temporal recurrence in favor of parallelizable self-attention mechanisms~\parencite{Vaswani_Shazeer_Parmar_Uszkoreit_Jones_Gomez_Kaiser_Polosukhin_2017}.


The core computational engine of the LLM is Scaled Dot-Product Attention. For a given input sequence, the model projects the embedded tokens into three distinct weight matrices: Queries ($Q$), Keys ($K$), and Values ($V$). The attention mechanism computes a weighted sum of the values, where the weight assigned to each value is determined by the dot-product similarity of the query with the corresponding key:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:self_attention}
    \text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V
\end{equation}
where $d_k$ is the dimension of the key vectors, utilized as a scaling factor to prevent vanishing gradients within the softmax function~\parencite{Vaswani_Shazeer_Parmar_Uszkoreit_Jones_Gomez_Kaiser_Polosukhin_2017}. In Causal Language Modeling (CLM), a strict lower-triangular mask is applied to the $Q K^T$ matrix. This ensures that the prediction for token $i$ can only attend to tokens at positions less than $i$, mathematically preserving the autoregressive property.


Given a context sequence $X$ and a target sequence of tokens $\mathbf{y} = (y_1, y_2, \dots, y_N)$, the network is optimized by minimizing the negative log-likelihood (Categorical Cross-Entropy) over the sequence~\parencite{Radford_Narasimhan_Salimans_Sutskever_2018}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clm_loss}
    L_{\text{CLM}} = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \log P(y_i \mid y_{<i}, X; \Theta)
\end{equation}
where $\Theta$ represents the parameters of the neural network. The probability $P(y_i)$ is computed by applying a softmax function over the final projection layer logits across the entire vocabulary dimension.

\subsection{Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (QLoRA)}
\label{sec:qlora}

Deploying raw foundation models within high-frequency MARL loops introduces prohibitive computational latency and memory constraints. Full parameter fine-tuning of billion-parameter models requires massive GPU clusters, rendering it infeasible for standard local execution. To mitigate this, this thesis utilizes Quantized Low-Rank Adaptation (QLoRA)~\parencite{Dettmers_Pagnoni_Holtzman_Zettlemoyer_2023}, a state-of-the-art Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) technique building upon the foundational Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) framework~\parencite{Hu_Shen_Wallis_Allen-Zhu_Li_Wang_Wang_Chen_2021}.


The mathematical intuition behind LoRA relies on the hypothesis that the updates to the weight matrices during fine-tuning possess an intrinsically low ``rank''. Instead of updating the full pre-trained weight matrix $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$, QLoRA freezes $W_0$ using 4-bit NormalFloat quantization. It then injects two trainable, low-rank adapter matrices $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}$ and $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$, where the rank $r \ll \min(d, k)$~\parencite{Hu_Shen_Wallis_Allen-Zhu_Li_Wang_Wang_Chen_2021}. 


To ensure that the untrained adapters do not inject noise into the network at the beginning of training, matrix $A$ is initialized with random Gaussian values, while matrix $B$ is initialized as a zero matrix. Consequently, the initial product $BA = 0$. The modified forward pass is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lora_forward}
    h = W_0 x + \Delta W x = W_0 x + \frac{\alpha}{r} (B A) x
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ is a constant scaling factor utilized to modulate the magnitude of the adapter activations~\parencite{Hu_Shen_Wallis_Allen-Zhu_Li_Wang_Wang_Chen_2021}.

The computational efficiency of this architecture is realized exclusively during backpropagation. When the loss $L_{\text{CLM}}$ is calculated against the target tokens, gradients are propagated backward through the network layers. Crucially, when the gradients reach the attention blocks, the gradients for the frozen matrix $W_0$ are discarded. The optimizer exclusively calculates and applies updates to the dense, low-dimensional adapter matrices $A$ and $B$. This targeted gradient flow prevents the caching of massive optimizer states, reducing overall VRAM consumption by up to 75\% compared to full fine-tuning.

\subsection{The Qwen Architecture and Agentic Capabilities}
\label{sec:qwen_architecture}

To serve as the cognitive controller in a MARL system, the chosen foundation model must possess advanced coding, reasoning, and instruction-following capabilities. This thesis leverages the Qwen model family, specifically utilizing the Qwen 2.5 7B parameters architecture, representing state-of-the-art open-weights performance~\parencite{Yang_Li_Yang_Zhang_Hui_Zheng_Yu_Gao_Huang_Lv_et_al._2025}.


A key innovation in the Qwen architecture is the inherent integration of a ``thinking mode'' for complex, multi-step reasoning alongside a non-thinking mode for rapid, context-driven responses~\parencite{Yang_Li_Yang_Zhang_Hui_Zheng_Yu_Gao_Huang_Lv_et_al._2025}. In the context of reinforcement learning, this duality is critical. The model can rapidly parse structured JSON inputs representing the environment state, while simultaneously allocating internal computational resources to process multi-step dependency paths (e.g., verifying that a Pickaxe must be crafted before Iron can be mined) without requiring separate, dedicated reasoning models. By coupling the Qwen architecture with QLoRA, the model can be locally fine-tuned to act as a highly specialized, latency-bound orchestrator.

\subsection{Neuro-Symbolic Orchestration and Verbal Reflection}
\label{sec:llm_rl_integration}

As established in Chapter 2, pure deep reinforcement learning struggles with severe exploration bottlenecks in sparse-reward environments. While frameworks such as Potential-Based Reward Shaping (PBRS) and the Options Framework offer mathematical solutions to temporal abstraction, designing the requisite potential functions $\Phi(s)$ or initiation sets $\mathcal{I}_o$ traditionally demands extensive human engineering. 


By leveraging the latent reasoning capabilities established during CLM pre-training, LLMs can act as zero-shot semantic orchestrators, bypassing manual heuristic design. Frameworks such as ReAct~\parencite{Yao_Zhao_Yu_Du_Shafran_Narasimhan_Cao_2023} demonstrate that interleaving language reasoning traces with concrete actions enables models to solve complex, multi-step tasks autonomously. In reinforcement learning specifically, systems like EUREKA~\parencite{ma2024eurekahumanlevelrewarddesign} and PRM4RL~\parencite{Zhang_Gao_Jiang_Chen_Pan_Zhang_Deng_2025} successfully utilize LLMs to autonomously write and refine continuous reward functions. Similarly, literature highlights an emergent paradigm of LLM-as-an-Orchestrator~\parencite{Sun_Huang_Pompili_2024}, decoupling strategic goal formulation from low-level kinetic execution.
