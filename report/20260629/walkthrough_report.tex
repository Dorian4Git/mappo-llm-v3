\section{Phase 2: Reward Shaping vs.\ Policy Shaping}

This section compares two fundamentally different mechanisms through which a Large Language Model can influence a cooperative MARL training loop.  The comparison is designed as a controlled ablation to determine \emph{how} the LLM's output is most effectively channelled.

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation}

\subsubsection{Reward Shaping (LLM-Dynamic)}

In the reward-shaping approach the LLM modifies the \textbf{reward signal} rather than the policy.
The LLM outputs raw logits $c_i$ for each of $K{=}7$ subtask weights. These are normalised into a strict budget\footnote{Normalisation is done via a temperature-scaled softmax function ($w_i = K \cdot \exp(c_i / \tau) / \sum \exp(c_j / \tau)$) with $\tau=0.5$ to ensure the total weight budget always sums to $K$. This prevents the LLM from simply assigning infinite weight to everything and hacking the reward signal.} before being passed to the planner.

A deterministic greedy planner then selects the goal with the highest weighted score:
\[
    g^* \;=\; \arg\max_{g \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{valid}}} \; R^{\text{base}}_g \;\cdot\; w_g
\]
and the shaped reward follows the standard potential-based form:
\[
    r^{\text{shaped}}_t \;=\; \gamma\,\varphi_{g^*}(s_{t+1}) \;-\; \varphi_{g^*}(s_t)
\]
The total reward is $r_t = r^{\text{env}}_t + \lambda_t \cdot r^{\text{shaped}}_t$, with $\lambda_t$ annealing from 1.0 to 0.

The key difference: the LLM does \textbf{not} select goals directly.  It only adjusts a weight vector, and the planner translates that into goal selection.

\subsubsection{Policy Shaping (HRL -- Options Framework)}

In the HRL approach the LLM acts as a \textbf{meta-controller}.
Given the current inventory state $s_t$, the LLM directly selects a discrete \emph{option} $o_i$ for each agent from a fixed set $\mathcal{O}$:
\[
    o_i \;=\; \pi_{\text{LLM}}(s_t), \qquad o_i \in \mathcal{O} = \{\texttt{COLLECT\_WOOD},\;\dots,\;\texttt{COLLECT\_GOLD},\;\texttt{IDLE}\}
\]
The low-level MAPPO policy $\pi_\theta$ receives the active option as a one-hot embedding appended to its observation vector. While the agent is working on the task, it receives a continuous \emph{dense shaping reward} based on its distance to the goal to guide it. Once it successfully completes the task, it receives a sparse success bonus of 1.0:
\[
    r^{\text{HRL}}_t \;=\;
    \begin{cases}
        1.0 & \text{if option } o_i \text{ succeeded at step } t,\\[4pt]
        \alpha\,\bigl(\gamma\,\varphi(s_{t+1}) - \varphi(s_t)\bigr) & \text{while working on the option (distance shaping)}
    \end{cases}
\]
where $\varphi(s) = D_{\max} - \lVert p_{\text{agent}} - p_{\text{goal}} \rVert_2$ is the potential function and $\alpha = 0.05$. This ensures the agent is constantly rewarded for moving closer to its assigned target, rather than only being rewarded at the very end.

The total reward is $r_t = r^{\text{env}}_t + 0.5\,r^{\text{HRL}}_t$. The LLM \textbf{constrains the action space}: agents can only pursue goals assigned by the meta-controller.  This is inherently stable because the MDP's reward distribution is consistent for a fixed option.

\subsection{Fairness of the Comparison}

Both systems have access to equivalent task-dependency knowledge:

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Dimension} & \textbf{HRL (Policy Shaping)} & \textbf{LLM-Dynamic (Reward Shaping)} \\
\midrule
DAG knowledge & Explicit in prompt text & Implicit in planner conditionals \\
LLM output    & Discrete option per agent & Continuous weight logits \\
Goal selection & LLM decides directly & Planner decides via $\arg\max$ \\
Stability      & High (fixed option $\Rightarrow$ fixed MDP) & Low (weight shifts $\Rightarrow$ non-stationary MDP) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Side-by-side comparison of the two LLM integration mechanisms.}
\end{table}

The \texttt{-{}-fair-mode} flag disables the programmatic DAG guardrails that would otherwise zero out weights for unreachable subtasks.  Both systems therefore rely on equivalent structural knowledge---one encoded in natural language, the other in the planner's inventory-based conditionals.

\subsection{Analysis: The Softmax Concentration Trap}

The LLM-Dynamic run exhibits a characteristic failure mode:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The LLM observes that Iron/Workbench are not yet saturated and increases their logits.
    \item At $\tau{=}0.5$, the softmax concentrates ${\sim}96\%$ of the budget onto these two weights ($w_{\text{iron}} \approx w_{\text{wb}} \approx 3.35$ out of a total budget of 7.0).
    \item With Bridge at $w \approx 0.06$, the planner score is $3.0 \times 0.06 = 0.18$, while Iron scores $2.0 \times 3.35 = 6.70$---agents \emph{never} target Bridge.
    \item Because Bridge/Enemy/Gold are never attempted, the LLM never observes progress on them and never reallocates budget. \textbf{Deadlock.}
\end{enumerate}

By contrast, the HRL system avoids this entirely: the LLM selects \texttt{BUILD\_BRIDGE} \emph{directly} once prerequisites are met, bypassing the $\arg\max$ bottleneck altogether.

This confirms the thesis hypothesis: \textbf{the mechanism of LLM influence---policy shaping versus reward shaping---is the critical differentiator}, not the availability of task-structure knowledge.
